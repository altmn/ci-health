# Optimize CI feedback and staging readiness

Use this playbook to choose, implement, and qualify a targeted improvement. For an audit or preflight, use the relevant sections to identify and explain opportunities. Follow [measurement](measurement.md) and [experiments](experiments.md) when collecting a baseline, quantifying benefit, or piloting a change.

## Define the fastest useful outcome

Start with the decision the developer needs and the revision or artifact it applies to. When staging is the goal, measure from the push to that candidate being deployed and ready in staging. Also track time to first actionable failure, time to a valid required verdict, and time to the production qualification decision where applicable. Include trigger/dispatch, approval, queue, setup, execution, artifact transfer, and readiness as applicable. Record superseded, failed, and never-ready candidates alongside comparable completions. Use p50/p95 and unresolved counts for feedback and mean occupied demand per candidate for capacity. Add the work done in other lanes, including fallback, audits, retries, selector overhead, and downstream effects.

Ask which checks must finish before each exposure. A fast staging lane can deliberately front-load high-signal checks and run broader qualification concurrently or at a later gate. For example, if project policy accepts a five-minute staging lane with 98% observed recall on representative known regressions, it can be preferable to a twenty-minute lane at 99.9%, provided each mandatory contract still completes before its applicable exposure. Treat those numbers as an example of a decision, never as targets, a measured result, or permission to change a gate. Define the regression denominator, severity classes, detection deadlines, and acceptable loss with the owner. Challenge severe failure modes individually. Include staging-side consequences such as exposed data, side effects, migrations, or shared environments when deciding what may wait.

Tie deferred evidence to the exact tested tree or artifact and the next gate, with a deadline, owner, freshness rule, and failure or rollback action. Exercise missing, late, stale, and failed evidence. Prior green on another candidate cannot qualify the current one. Changing a mandatory contract or protection requires explicit authority. A faster pipeline can retain all current policy by eliminating waste or shortening the urgent path first.

## Choose the highest-leverage change

Trace the real dependency graph through task wrappers, artifact consumers, test setup, and job results. Inspect frequent and expensive paths and the slowest queue and execution tails. Look for work you can stop doing, requirements you can prove more cheaply, and checks that can finish outside the urgent path. Rank feasible options by expected impact on the target decision, demand, detectable regressions, rollout cost, and reversibility. Trace deeper when it would change the choice. Implement the highest-leverage authorized change supported by the available evidence, including structural changes when the evidence supports them. A static invariant can justify a narrow edit; it cannot establish observed latency or cost.

| Observed mechanism | Changes to consider and what to check |
|---|---|
| Duplicate triggers, superseded runs, repeated builds or setup | Collapse equivalent work, cancel safely obsolete attempts, batch short setup-heavy jobs, or build once and share a verified artifact. Account for genuine event contracts, trust boundaries, provisioning, artifact transfer, and different runner classes. [Orchestration](orchestration.md) · [Reuse](selection-and-reuse.md#cache-and-result-reuse) |
| Artificial serialization or late failure | Remove prerequisites that carry no artifact, state, safety requirement, or verdict. Preserve real dependencies and required aggregation. Start cheap high-signal checks early. Measure queue and contention before widening fanout. [Orchestration](orchestration.md) |
| Long tail on uneven shards | Replay a stable inventory with per-file or per-case durations, then pilot an improved partition under representative capacity. Check the slowest item, setup cost, total demand, and runner contention. [Shard inventories](selection-and-reuse.md#inventory-and-shard-invariants) |
| Broad testing for narrow changes | Use an affected graph, ownership or observed dependencies and protected default checks, with broader fallbacks. Consider moving equivalent assertions to a cheaper layer or moving broader checks to a named stage under an explicit detection policy. [Affected selection](selection-and-reuse.md#qualify-affected-selection) |
| Install, compile, browser or service overhead | Profile called commands, remove repeated starts, reuse trustworthy outputs, test faster tools separately, and evaluate runner sizing against setup and queue costs. For compatible JavaScript or TypeScript workloads, see the [optional Bun recipe](bun-ci.md). [Execution](execution.md) |
| Flakes, retries, or slow failure diagnosis | Preserve the first report, fix dominant flaky signatures, and retry only classified transient failures with bounds and observable outcomes. Route actionable failure evidence to the owner promptly. Include any log analysis or retry overhead in the improvement. [Execution](execution.md) |

### Remove a false prerequisite or duplicate job

Map the produced and consumed artifacts, environmental state, privileges, concurrency keys, and required outcomes on both paths. Trace the exact workflow events and merge candidates. Remove a false edge or consolidate equivalent work while preserving necessary dependencies, reports, and a terminal required verdict. Verify with representative success, upstream failures, intentional skips, cancellation, and merge-group cases where applicable. A configured saving can be reported as jobs or edges removed. Measure time and demand from comparable runs. [GitHub required checks](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/how-tos/merge-and-close-pull-requests/troubleshooting-required-status-checks)

### Rebalance a suite

Freeze the canonical inventory, runner count/class, and a timing snapshot from comparable complete reports. Replay current and alternative plans on the same inputs. Use longest-first greedy placement or the runner's supported planner and compare the maximum weighted load and setup overhead. If one file dominates, inspect its safe split points before adding runners. Pin the same planner input across shards and reconcile each entry from the plan to its terminal report. Pilot with representative queueing, cache and peak conditions.

Include the missing-timing policy in the proposed plan. For example, place a new file using an estimate from similar files and refresh its weight after a complete run. If the profile is missing or too stale to trust, use a common complete fallback partition. Describe replay estimates separately from measured required-verdict latency and demand. [Shard invariants](selection-and-reuse.md#inventory-and-shard-invariants)

Before recommending the partition, recalculate each reported load from its exact assigned members plus setup. Reconcile total work and inventory against the baseline and check that prose, tables and any edited manifest describe the same final plan. Include the missing/new/stale timing policy in the delivered plan, rather than leaving it implicit in this reference.

### Qualify a faster selection or stage policy

Pin the tested tree and comparison base that carry the policy, including combined merge candidates where applicable. Challenge renames, deletions, global inputs, external fixtures, code generation, dynamic edges, and explicit empty selections. Use the [negative gate cases](selection-and-reuse.md#negative-gate-cases) to prevent a missing report or planner error from producing a green. Run a shadow lane against complete qualification on the same candidate to measure membership and omitted failures, then exercise selected-only execution to expose hidden setup or ordering dependencies. Use known regression episodes, severity, and remaining uncertainty when choosing a route. Account for selector freshness, audit traffic, unvalidated queue growth, and fallback cost. Escalate ambiguous cases to broader validation or surface a blocked decision.

## Implement, verify, and decide

Use the repository's normal entry points and narrow validation for workflow syntax, command paths, and failure behavior. Validate live platform semantics when the claim depends on settings, merge queues, capacity, or protected gates. When those observations are unavailable, record the unverified outcomes and keep the change in a reversible or shadow state where needed. Diagnose/recommendation requests remain read-only. Implement requested local fixes and carry them through the authorized verification scope.

Compare representative baseline and candidate runs using the agreed population and thresholds. Include cold and warm paths, failures and peak conditions relevant to the change. Check that latency gains reach the required decision or staging readiness, that physical work removed exceeds added overhead, and that detection and proof remain within policy. If the objective trades time, demand, and detection, show the measured cost of each option and which trade the owner authorized. Record the exact changed mechanism, validation status, rollout and rollback trigger, and any pending evidence. [Experiment and rollout procedure](experiments.md)

The handoff must name the remaining acceptance check, its endpoint/conditions and a concrete stop or rollback trigger when validation is pending. Passing syntax or offline gate tests alone does not close a live latency or exposure requirement. Keep this closure condition in the final report so the next caller can act on it.
